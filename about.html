I analyse where industrial carbon comes from, where it can be stored, and what it costs to get it there.

I’m Try Hutomo Abednego, a chemical engineer working on decarbonisation in Indonesia. I spent four years on the ethylene cold section of a naphtha cracker, nearly three modelling upstream emissions and carbon capture across Asia-Pacific for S&P Global, and now work on climate risk, carbon markets and the net-zero roadmap at Chandra Asri Pacific. Carbon Bender is where I write about it.

Jakarta, Indonesia

Focus

Petrochemical decarbonisation
Process-level options for steam crackers and their derivatives: electrification, fuel switching and carbon capture, and the life cycle accounting needed to compare them fairly.
Carbon capture and storage
Source-to-sink screening, storage capacity and project economics, from acid gas field developments to shared industrial hubs in Southeast Asia and Australia.
Carbon markets
Registries, methodologies and price outlooks for compliance and voluntary credits, and what it takes for an industrial company to participate in them.
Climate risk and disclosure
Scenario-based physical and transition risk, water stress, GHG inventories, and reporting against TCFD, IFRS S2, CDP and SBTi.

Most of my work sits where process engineering meets carbon economics: whether an abatement option works on a real plant, and what it is worth once regulation and carbon prices are counted.
